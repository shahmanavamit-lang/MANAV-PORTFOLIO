RAG-Based Citation-Grounded Document Q&A System

Retrieval-augmented generation · Citation verification

Read the full paper ↗View on Zenodo ↗
Shah Manav Amit · DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.22685189

Hybrid retrieval meets claim-level verification. An end-to-end document Q&A system that checks whether cited evidence supports the generated answer.

The question

What does it take to make a generated answer trustworthy? This project focuses on the gap between attaching a citation and checking that the cited passage actually supports the claim.

The approach

The system parses PDFs, splits their text into overlapping chunks, and builds both a FAISS vector index and a BM25 keyword index. Reciprocal Rank Fusion combines the two retrieval results before an LLM produces a source-grounded answer.

A second step separates the answer into claim–citation pairs and checks each pair for entailment. The interface distinguishes verified, unsupported, and unverified citations, while the generation stage is instructed to decline questions the document cannot answer.

What the evaluation showed

The paper reports results on a 14-question hand-labelled set: 10 answerable questions and 4 deliberately unanswerable ones. Retrieval Hit Rate@3, refusal accuracy, and citation structural validity each reached 100% on this set; claim-level citation faithfulness was 80% (8/10).

These are results from a small project evaluation, rather than a broad benchmark. The remaining faithfulness gap, larger evaluations, scanned PDFs, and document-size edge cases are identified as areas for further work.

From research to implementation

The implementation uses Python, PyMuPDF, sentence-transformers, FAISS, BM25, Ollama with Llama 3.2, and Streamlit. It supports local inference and an alternative OpenAI-compatible API.
